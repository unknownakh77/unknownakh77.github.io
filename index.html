Abstract

This term I worked as a Security Analyst Intern on KPMG's Digital Security team in Toronto, helping secure the firm's own internal environment across a stack of enterprise security tools. What follows isn't a client debrief — it's a look at what the day-to-day of an internal security analyst actually involves, what I built on the side, and where my own goals held up against the reality of the work.

The Employer

KPMG — Digital Security, ITS

KPMG is one of the "Big 4" professional services firms. Rather than the client-facing Risk Services practice, I sat inside ITS (Information Technology Services) — the internal group responsible for securing KPMG's own environment, not its clients'. That distinction shaped the work: fewer client deliverables, more direct ownership of the firm's own security posture.

My term was based out of the Toronto office, working within the Digital Security team on internal detection, access, and endpoint security operations.

The Job

Inside the day-to-day

Day to day, my hands-on work centered on three platforms: Microsoft Defender for Endpoint for threat detection and response, Qualys for vulnerability scanning, and ServiceNow for tracking it all through a formal ticketing workflow.

Microsoft Defender for Endpoint Qualys ServiceNow

Beyond that daily toolkit, I also got exposure to the platforms that sit around it in a mature security stack — Microsoft Sentinel for centralized detection, CyberArk for privileged access management, and Symantec DLP for data loss prevention. I didn't operate these day to day, but understanding how they connect to endpoint detection and vulnerability management filled in the bigger picture of how a security team's tools work together.

Microsoft Sentinel CyberArk Symantec DLP

The steepest part of the learning curve wasn't any single tool — it was the volume and pace. A university lab environment has one or two moving parts; an internal enterprise environment has dozens, and most of the actual skill is learning to prioritize signal under that load.

Omni Cortex — an AI productivity tool

Alongside client work, I built Omni Cortex, an AI-powered productivity tool, and entered it into KPMG's internal AI Innovation Competition — open to interns across the firm.

Conclusion

What I'd want someone to remember

If I had to sum up the term in one line: I went in wanting to see what security work looks like at real scale, and came out having built something that stood on its own against 500 other interns' ideas. The technical tools will keep changing — the instinct for triage, prioritization, and building something extra when there's room to, is what I'm taking into the next term.
